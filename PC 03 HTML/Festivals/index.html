FESTIVALS

Holiday homework by Rashi — A short assignment on major Indian festivals.

1. DUSSEHRA

Dussehra, also known as Vijayadashami, marks the victory of good over evil.

It commemorates Lord Rama’s triumph over Ravana and the goddess Durga’s victory over Mahishasura in different traditions.

Across India, people watch Ramlila plays and burn towering effigies of Ravana, Meghnad, and Kumbhkaran.

The festival concludes the nine days of Navratri and welcomes a season of new beginnings.

Cities like Mysuru and Kullu are famous for grand processions and cultural performances.

Families gather for prayers, community events, and celebrations that emphasize courage and righteousness.

Image: Dussehra effigy burning ceremony.

2. DIWALI

Diwali, the Festival of Lights, symbolizes the victory of light over darkness and knowledge over ignorance.

Homes are decorated with diyas, rangoli, and lanterns, creating a warm and joyful atmosphere.

People worship Lakshmi and Ganesha for prosperity and new beginnings.

Families exchange sweets, gifts, and visit relatives to strengthen bonds.

In many regions, Diwali also commemorates Lord Rama’s return to Ayodhya after exile.

It’s a time for cleaning, renewal, and community celebrations with fireworks and cultural events.

Image: Diyas and rangoli during Diwali.

3. HOLI

Holi is the vibrant festival of colors, celebrating joy, unity, and the arrival of spring.

The night before, Holika Dahan bonfires symbolize the burning of evil and the power of faith.

On the main day, people throw gulal (colored powders), dance, and share festive foods.

Holi bridges social gaps, inviting everyone to participate with laughter and friendship.

Popular dishes include gujiya and thandai, adding flavor to the festivities.

Music, street celebrations, and community events make Holi a lively, inclusive experience.

Image: People celebrating Holi with colors.

4. INDEPENDENCE DAY

Independence Day is observed on 15 August to honor India’s freedom from colonial rule.

Ceremonies include flag hoisting, patriotic songs, and cultural programs in schools and communities.

The Prime Minister addresses the nation from the Red Fort, reflecting on progress and aspirations.

Streets and homes shine with tricolor decorations and national pride.

It’s a day to remember the sacrifices of freedom fighters and reaffirm democratic values.

Students participate in parades, speeches, and activities that celebrate unity and diversity.

Image: Flag hoisting on Independence Day.

5. REPUBLIC DAY

Republic Day, celebrated on 26 January, marks the adoption of the Constitution of India.

The grand parade in New Delhi showcases India’s cultural heritage and military strength.

Tableaux from different states display traditions, innovations, and achievements.

Schools organize programs with speeches, patriotic songs, and awards.

It’s a day to honor the values of justice, liberty, equality, and fraternity.

Citizens reflect on their responsibilities and the spirit of democracy.
